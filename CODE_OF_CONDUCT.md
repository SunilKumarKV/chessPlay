# ChessPlay Code of Conduct

## Purpose
This Code of Conduct details our standards of behavior, scope of enforcement, and procedures for reporting violations to maintain a welcoming, respectful, and secure community.

## Our Commitment
In the interest of fostering an open and welcoming environment, we as contributors and maintainers pledge to make participation in our project and our community a harassment-free experience for everyone, regardless of age, body size, disability, ethnicity, sex characteristics, gender identity and expression, level of experience, education, socio-economic status, nationality, personal appearance, race, religion, or sexual identity and orientation.

---

## Standards of Behavior

### Expected Behavior
- **Be Professional and Respectful**: Treat all community members with courtesy.
- **Collaborate Openly**: Welcome constructive critique and diverse technical perspectives.
- **Graceful Acceptance of Feedback**: Focus on technical improvements and user-oriented quality.
- **Protect Community Security**: Keep private information confidential and handle vulnerability disclosures through private channels.

### Unacceptable Behavior
- **Harassment and Discrimination**: Trolling, insults, personal attacks, or discriminatory jokes.
- **Exposing Secrets**: Committing API keys, environment settings, or personal details to public threads.
- **Exploitative Behavior**: Deliberately using ChessPlay communication channels to spread malware, spam, or advertise unauthorized products.
- **Public Vulnerability Disclosures**: Publicly releasing zero-day exploits before private mitigation can take place.

---

## Examples

### 1. Constructive Technical Feedback
* **Acceptable**: *"The socket validation layer at `backend/src/sockets/game.ts` doesn't enforce bounds check on the board length. Let's add a range validator before calling `chess.js` functions."*
* **Unacceptable**: *"Your code is completely broken and insecure, why did you write this garbage validation loop?"*

### 2. Community Engagement
* **Acceptable**: *"We recommend checking our setup documentation before configuring Postgres locally; we use pnpm to handle multi-module installs."*
* **Unacceptable**: *"RTFM. If you can't install pnpm, you shouldn't be contributing here."*

## Notes
- > [!IMPORTANT]
  > This Code of Conduct applies both within project spaces and in public spaces when an individual is representing the project or its community.
- > [!CAUTION]
  > Deliberate security bypasses or introducing backdoors disguised as commits will result in immediate and permanent bans from the organization.

---

## Best Practices
- **Lead by Example**: Show empathy and support to newcomers.
- **De-escalate Arguments**: If conversations become heated, take a step back and stick strictly to technical facts.
- **Report Early**: If you witness unacceptable behavior, report it immediately to the maintainers instead of engaging.
